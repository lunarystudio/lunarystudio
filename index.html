Welcome to the world of Lunary

Lunary is a mail-order studio for people who still believe the sky keeps a calendar. Once a month an envelope leaves the building: a signed edition, a chart for the weeks ahead, and one small instruction for paying attention.

No feed. No algorithm. No scroll. Just paper that arrives when it said it would — addressed by hand, sealed at the studio, made for the version of you that reads in the evening.

The studio  ·  Envelope 07 is on the press

We make twelve things a year. That is the whole plan.

Lunary Studios is two people, a press, and a calendar borrowed from the sky. Nobody here has ever asked how to increase output, because the answer is always the same: wait for the moon.

Paper is slow, and that is not a flaw.

The studio began with a shelf of unsent letters and one question: what would art look like if it arrived at the speed of the sky instead of the speed of a feed? The answer took a month to work out and has taken every month since.

So an envelope leaves on each full moon. Fifty of each, hand-addressed, carrying a signed work, the chart for the weeks ahead, and one instruction for paying attention. When the fiftieth is gone the plate is scratched and the ink is not mixed again.

If it could arrive instantly, it would not be worth sending.

Nothing here ships on a Tuesday because a warehouse had a free slot.

The press runs when the work is ready and the envelopes post when the moon is full. That means roughly twelve dispatches a year, occasionally thirteen, and a studio that is deliberately quiet in between.

How an issue is made

Four stages, in this order, every month. Skipping one is how you get a poster instead of an issue.

01

Drawn

The new moon starts with a blank page and one question worth a month of work. Most questions are discarded here, which is the point of starting early.

02

Proofed

At the first quarter the plate goes down three times. Two of the proofs usually fail — wrong pressure, wrong paper, or an idea that only worked in the sketchbook.

03

Printed

Fifty impressions, pulled by hand, inked and registered one at a time. Each is signed and numbered because after the fiftieth the plate is retired for good.

04

Posted

Envelopes are addressed in ink, sealed with wax, and handed to the post on the full moon. Then the studio goes quiet and the next question begins.

The ledger

What actually goes into an envelope, and into a year. Written down here because a promise you cannot check is not a promise.
